# Glossary

## Absorption

The process by which a material takes in light energy.

In lasers, absorption occurs when an atom or ion captures a photon and moves to a higher energy level.

Related:
- Stimulated Emission
- Pumping

---

## Beam Quality

A measure of how closely a real laser beam behaves like an ideal Gaussian beam.

Beam quality is commonly described using the **M² factor**.

Lower values indicate higher beam quality.

---

## Coherence

The degree to which light waves maintain a predictable phase relationship.

Laser light has much higher coherence than ordinary light.

Two types are commonly discussed:

- Temporal coherence
- Spatial coherence

---

## Dopant

An intentionally added impurity introduced into a host material to provide laser-active ions.

Examples include:

- Neodymium
- Ytterbium
- Erbium
- Holmium

---

## Electric Field

One of the two oscillating fields that make up an electromagnetic wave.

Measured in volts per meter (V/m).

---

## Electromagnetic Radiation

Energy that propagates through space as oscillating electric and magnetic fields.

Examples include:

- Radio waves
- Microwaves
- Infrared
- Visible light
- Ultraviolet
- X-rays

---

## Electromagnetic Wave

A self-propagating oscillation consisting of mutually perpendicular electric and magnetic fields.

Light is an electromagnetic wave.

---

## Frequency

The number of complete wave cycles passing a point every second.

Measured in hertz (Hz).

Relationship:

$$
c=f\lambda
$$

---

## Gain Medium

The material inside a laser that amplifies light through stimulated emission.

Examples:

- Nd:YAG
- Yb:YAG
- Er:YAG
- Fiber gain media

---

## Host Material

The crystal, glass, ceramic, or fiber into which laser-active ions are introduced.

Examples:

- YAG
- YLF
- Silica
- Sapphire

---

## Intensity

Optical power divided by beam area.

Typical units:

- W/m²
- W/cm²
- W/mm²

Higher intensity generally increases interaction with materials.

---

## Laser

An acronym for:

**Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation**

A laser generates coherent, highly directional, nearly monochromatic light.

---

## Magnetic Field

The second oscillating field that makes up an electromagnetic wave.

Measured in tesla (T).

---

## Monochromatic

Light consisting of a very narrow range of wavelengths.

Real lasers are not perfectly monochromatic but often come very close.

---

## Optical Power

The rate at which optical energy is delivered.

Measured in watts (W).

---

## Photon

The smallest discrete packet (quantum) of electromagnetic energy.

Photons carry energy proportional to their frequency.

Future lessons derive:

$$
E=hf
$$

---

## Polarization

The orientation of the electric field of a light wave.

Laser polarization strongly affects:

- Reflection
- Absorption
- Nonlinear optics
- Beam combining

---

## Population Inversion

A condition where more atoms occupy an excited state than a lower-energy state.

Population inversion is required for conventional laser operation.

---

## Pumping

Adding energy to the gain medium to create population inversion.

Common methods include:

- Diode pumping
- Flashlamp pumping
- Electrical pumping

---

## Resonator

An arrangement of mirrors that repeatedly reflects light through the gain medium to build optical amplification.

Often called an **optical cavity**.

---

## Spatial Coherence

The consistency of phase across different parts of a laser beam.

High spatial coherence enables tight focusing.

---

## Stimulated Emission

The process in which an incoming photon causes an excited atom to emit a second photon.

The emitted photon has:

- the same wavelength
- the same phase
- the same direction
- the same polarization

This is the physical principle behind every conventional laser.

---

## Temporal Coherence

The consistency of phase over time.

Temporal coherence determines coherence length and spectral linewidth.

---

## Wavelength

The distance between repeating points on a wave.

Represented by:

$$
\lambda
$$

Common units:

- nm
- μm
- m
