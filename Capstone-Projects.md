# Capstone Engineering Projects

> **Purpose:**  
> The capstone sequence is the culmination of the **Laser Materials & Solid-State Lasers Masterclass**. Rather than testing memorization, each project requires you to design, justify, defend, and refine complete laser systems using the principles learned throughout the course.
>
> Every capstone simulates a professional engineering design review. You will be expected to justify every major design decision with physics, mathematics, engineering tradeoffs, manufacturability, reliability, safety, and cost considerations.

# Overview

The capstone projects transition from guided engineering exercises to open-ended system design.

Each project builds on previous projects while introducing new engineering constraints.

By the end of the sequence, you should be capable of designing a commercially viable laser system from first principles.

---

# Learning Objectives

Upon completing the capstone sequence, you should be able to:

- Select an appropriate gain medium.
- Justify dopant concentration.
- Select pump sources.
- Design resonators.
- Estimate thermal loading.
- Choose cooling systems.
- Evaluate nonlinear optics.
- Estimate beam quality.
- Estimate efficiency.
- Compare manufacturing options.
- Evaluate reliability.
- Identify failure risks.
- Design for maintainability.
- Address laser safety requirements.
- Defend engineering decisions during formal design reviews.

---

# Engineering Design Philosophy

Every project follows one guiding principle:

> There is no perfect laser.

There are only:

- better tradeoffs
- worse tradeoffs

Your goal is to demonstrate sound engineering judgment rather than maximize one specification.

---

# Design Review Process

Each capstone follows the same workflow.

## Phase 1 — Requirements

Define:

- Customer
- Application
- Budget
- Performance requirements
- Environmental requirements
- Regulatory requirements

---

## Phase 2 — Concept Selection

Choose:

- Laser architecture
- Gain medium
- Dopant(s)
- Pump source
- Pump geometry
- Cooling approach

Justify every selection.

---

## Phase 3 — Preliminary Design

Develop:

- Optical layout
- Resonator
- Beam delivery
- Thermal design
- Mechanical packaging
- Power requirements

---

## Phase 4 — Engineering Analysis

Estimate:

- Output wavelength
- Output power
- Pulse energy (if applicable)
- Beam quality (M²)
- Efficiency
- Thermal load
- Reliability
- Manufacturing complexity

---

## Phase 5 — Design Review

Present your design.

Expect critical questions regarding:

- Cost
- Reliability
- Manufacturability
- Safety
- Failure modes
- Supply chain
- Future scalability

---

## Phase 6 — Revision

Revise your design based on review feedback.

Iteration is expected.

# Final Master Design Review

The final assessment combines everything learned throughout the course.

You will receive a realistic engineering specification from a fictional customer.

No solution will be suggested.

You must independently:

- Interpret requirements
- Make assumptions
- Design the system
- Perform calculations
- Defend tradeoffs
- Address safety
- Estimate cost
- Respond to engineering review questions

After presenting your design, it will be critiqued using the evaluation rubric. You may then revise and resubmit, mirroring an actual engineering development cycle.
